# Day 5 — Final Project & Showcase: "Show What You Know"
**Theme:** Plan it, build it, code it, present it.
 
## 🎯 Learning Objectives
By the end of Day 5, kids should be able to:
- Apply sequences, loops, and conditionals in one connected project
- Collaborate with their group to divide and complete roles
- Present their work clearly and confidently
- Give and receive peer feedback respectfully
- Reflect on their full week of learning
---
 
## 10:00–10:20 | Welcome & Project Launch
 
**Facilitator script:**
> "This is it. Five days ago you didn't know what an algorithm was. Today, you're going to build something, code something, and present it — just like real programmers do."
 
Remind kids of everything they've learned:
- Day 1: Sequences — step-by-step instructions
- Day 2: Loops — repeating patterns
- Day 3: Conditionals — IF-THEN decisions
- Day 4: Events + Animation — bringing things to life
**Project brief:** Each group creates a **LEGO stack story** and a matching **Blockly animation** that tells the same story. The project must include:
- At least one **sequence** (actions in a deliberate order)
- At least one **loop** (something that repeats)
- At least one **conditional** (an IF-THEN decision)
- A **beginning, middle, and end**
Groups use the story prompt they chose or developed in Day 4. If they didn't settle on one, they pick from a facilitator-provided list (examples below).
 
**Example story prompts:**
- A robot discovers a new planet and has to decide if it's safe
- A lion cub crosses Lagos traffic to find its family
- A young coder builds a machine that goes out of control
- A team of friends finds a mysterious LEGO tower in the forest
---
 
## 10:20–11:00 | Build + Code Final Project
 
Groups work simultaneously on two things:
 
### LEGO Build
The physical scene — 3 stacked arrangements representing Beginning, Middle, End. Characters are brick stacks. The group labels each scene with a sticky note or marker card describing what is happening and what event triggers the transition.
 
### Blockly Animation (Free Sandbox)
The Blockly Final Project tool is a **full sandbox** — all blocks from Days 1–4 are available:
- Place brick blocks (sequencing)
- Repeat block (loops)
- IF-colour-then blocks (conditionals)
- Event blocks: When key pressed
- Action blocks: move, say, change costume, change background, play sound
Kids use any combination to animate their story. There are no levels, no targets — just a blank stage and all the tools. The only requirement is that their animation can be demonstrated live during the showcase.
 
**Suggested group roles:**
- **Builder:** leads the LEGO scene construction
- **Coder:** leads the Blockly animation
- **Narrator:** writes down what the story is about and practises the presentation
- **Director:** makes decisions when the group disagrees, keeps the group on track
- **Reporter:** gives the presentation to the class
*(For groups of 5, the Director and Reporter can be the same person, or the whole group presents together.)*
 
**Facilitator rotation tips:**
- Ask every group: "Do you have a beginning, middle, and end?"
- Ask the coder: "Where is your loop? Where is your IF?"
- Ask the builder: "Which stack represents your character? How does it change between scenes?"
- If a group is stuck: "Pick one scene. Just code what happens when the Left button is pressed in that scene. Start there."

## 11:00–11:30 | Presentations & Peer Applause
 
Each group gets **3–5 minutes.** The format:
 
1. Show the LEGO build — describe each scene: "This is the beginning. Our character is…"
2. Run the Blockly animation — narrate live as they press buttons
3. Explain the code concepts used: "We used a loop here because… our IF says…"
**Presentation prompts on the board:**
- "This is what we built…"
- "Our story is about…"
- "We used a loop to…"
- "When the player presses [key], our character…"
- "Our IF rule says: IF ___, THEN ___"
**Peer feedback — 2 Stars and a Wish:**
After each group presents, the class gives:
- ⭐ "I loved the way you…"
- ⭐ "Your story was really creative because…"
- 🌠 "I wish you had added…"
Facilitators model this for the first group so kids know the tone — kind, specific, curious.

## 11:50–12:00 | Final Badges & Certificate Moment
 
🏅 **Badge of the Day: "Code Champion"**
 
Earned by:
- ✅ Completing a team project with a LEGO build and Blockly animation
- ✅ Presenting to the group
- ✅ Using at least one loop or conditional in the final project
- ✅ Giving peer feedback to another group
**🎓 Certificate:**
Award paper certificates to every child with their name and the line:
*"For coding with courage and creativity — Dream Beyond Limits 2026."*
 
Facilitators sign each certificate. If possible, call each child's name individually to receive theirs.
 
**Closing message:**
> "Five days ago, you didn't know what a loop was. Now you can build one, explain one, and use one to tell a story. You are Instruction Explorers. Loop Masters. Decision Makers. Creative Coders. Code Champions. That is who you are now — and nobody can take that away from you. We cannot wait to see what you build next."

## Volunteer Notes — Day 5
 
- **Timing is tight.** 10 groups × 3–5 min = up to 50 minutes of presentations. Keep groups to 3 minutes if time is short; reserve the full 5 for groups with more to show.
- **Tech backup:** If a device fails during a presentation, the group can present their LEGO build alone and describe what their animation does. The code is the thinking, not just the screen.
- **Certificates:** Print and sign these the night before. Having a physical certificate to take home is significant for kids in this community.
- **Photographs:** If families or press are present, get consent before photographing. Prioritise group photos over individual close-ups.
