# Day 4 — Animation & Collaboration: "Plan It. Build It. Animate It."
**Theme:** Together, we bring characters to life.
 
## 🎯 Learning Objectives
By the end of Day 4, kids should be able to:
- Understand that animation is a sequence of changes triggered by events
- Plan a story with a beginning, middle, and end
- Use LEGO stacks to represent scenes and characters
- Build an interactive animated story in Blockly using events, movement, costumes, and sounds
- Share and remix a teammate's story

## 10:15–10:35 | Mini Lesson — What Is Animation?
 
**Facilitator talk points:**
- Animation = making something *change* over time. A character moves. A scene shifts. A face changes expression.
- In code, animation is a **sequence of actions triggered by events.** The character doesn't move until *something happens* — a key press, a click, a timer.
- "What's your favourite animated show or game? What makes it feel alive?" *(movement, reaction, sound, colour change)*

## 10:35–11:00 | LEGO Challenge — "3-Scene Stack Story"
 
This is the storyboarding activity. Kids plan their animation before touching a computer — the same way professional animators and game designers work.
 
**Setup:** Each group gets their bricks and a printed **3-panel storyboard sheet** (3 boxes labelled: Beginning / Middle / End).
 
### Instructions:
1. **Decide on a character.** The character is represented by a specific stack of bricks — a "character stack." For example: a 3-brick stack with yellow on top might be the lion.
2. **Build 3 scenes.** Each scene is a small arrangement of stacks — character position, surroundings, what is happening.
   - Scene 1 (Beginning): Where is the character? What is the situation?
   - Scene 2 (Middle): Something changes — the character moves, meets someone, faces a problem.
   - Scene 3 (End): How is it resolved? What changed?
3. **Fill in the storyboard sheet.** Draw or describe what each scene looks like. Label what *event* triggers the change between scenes:
   > "When [event] → scene changes to [next scene]."
**Why this works with stacking:** Each scene is a *state* — a specific arrangement of bricks. Moving from one scene to the next is a *change of state*, which is exactly what animation code does. The physical stacking makes the abstract concept of "state change" concrete.
 
**Facilitator tip:** Walk around and ask each group: "What is the event that moves you from Scene 1 to Scene 2? What button would trigger that?"

## 11:00–11:25 | Blockly Activity — Story Animator
 
**What the activity should look like:**
 
Kids open the Story Animator. The right side of the screen has a **stage** — a simple scene with an emoji character. Below the stage are **5 buttons**: Left, Right, Up, Down, Space. The workspace has blocks in 5 categories:
 
- **Events:** "When LEFT pressed," "When RIGHT pressed," "When UP pressed," "When DOWN pressed," "When SPACE pressed"
- **Move:** move left, move right, jump, spin
- **Speak:** say [message] — message chosen from a dropdown
- **Look:** change costume to [emoji], change background to [scene]
- **Sound:** play sound [clap / beep / cheer / woosh]
Kids drag an event block, then stack action blocks inside it. When they click Run, the character sits idle until a button is pressed — then the coded actions fire.
 
There are 4 levels:
- **Level 1:** Code Left and Right buttons — move the character across the stage.
- **Level 2:** Add Up (say something) and Down (change costume). Now 4 buttons each do something different.
- **Level 3:** Add Space to change the background. The scene shifts. Character + background together tell a story.
- **Level 4:** Free story mode. All 5 buttons unlocked. Kids recreate their LEGO storyboard in the animator — the sequence of button presses *is* the story. They present it to the group by narrating as they press buttons.
**The connection to the LEGO activity:** The 3 scenes kids planned physically now map directly to button presses — pressing Space changes the background to the next scene, Left/Right moves the character, Up makes them speak. The storyboard *becomes* the code plan.
 
**Facilitator prompt:**
> "Can you press the buttons in order and tell your story out loud while it plays? That's what a narrator does in an animated film."

## 11:25–11:45 | Remix Challenge — Group Collaboration
 
Groups pair up and swap storyboard sheets.
 
**Challenge:**
- Each group builds one **new scene** based on their partner's story — either an alternative ending, a surprise twist, or an extra character.
- OR: they animate their partner's story in the Blockly tool and add one element that wasn't in the original.
**Facilitator prompts:**
- "What did your team add that wasn't in the original version?"
- "Did you make your partner's story even cooler?"
- "In real coding teams, this is called a remix or a pull request — someone takes your idea and builds on it."
