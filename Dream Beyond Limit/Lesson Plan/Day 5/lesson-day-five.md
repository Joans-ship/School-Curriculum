Day 5 — Final Project & Showcase: "Show What You Know"

Theme: Build it, code it, present it. This is your moment.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of Day 5, kids should be able to:


Apply sequences, loops, and conditionals in a single connected project
Translate a cardboard storyboard plan into a LEGO build and Blockly animation
Present their work confidently using their physical props
Give and receive peer feedback respectfully
Reflect on their full week of learning



10:00–10:15 | Welcome Back + Cardboard Finish Time

Facilitator script:


"This is it. You planned your story yesterday. Today you build it, code it, and present it to everyone. Let's start by finishing anything left from your cardboard."



Groups retrieve their storyboard panels and character cutouts. Any group that didn't finish their panels or cutouts on Day 4 gets the first 10–15 minutes to complete them. Groups that finished use this time to rehearse narrating their story using the cardboard.

Facilitator check with every group:


"Do you have all 3 panels labelled?"
"Do you have your character cutout ready to hold up?"
"Can you tell me your story in three sentences — beginning, middle, end?"



10:15–10:55 | Build + Code Final Project

Groups now translate their cardboard plan into two deliverables simultaneously.

LEGO Build

Using their character stacks from Day 4 as a starting point, each group arranges their bricks into 3 scene setups — one per storyboard panel. Each scene sits next to its matching cardboard panel so the physical and drawn versions tell the same story side by side.

Groups label each brick scene with a small card: what is happening, what code concept is present (sequence, loop, or conditional).

Blockly Animation — Free Sandbox

The Day 5 Blockly tool is a full open sandbox — all blocks from Days 1–4 available with no levels, no targets. Kids open it and build freely from their storyboard plan.

All blocks available:


Place brick blocks (sequencing)
Repeat block (loops)
IF light-colour-then blocks (conditionals)
Event blocks: When key pressed (Left, Right, Up, Down, Space)
Action blocks: move, say, change costume, change background, play sound


The only requirement: their animation must be demonstrable live during the showcase. It does not need to be complete or polished — one working event is enough to show.

Suggested group roles:


Builder — leads the LEGO scene arrangement
Coder — leads the Blockly animation
Narrator — practises the spoken story using the cardboard panels
Props Manager — holds and moves the character cutout during the presentation
Director — keeps the group on track, settles disagreements


Facilitator rotation prompts:


"Where is your loop? Point it to me in your code."
"Which event triggers the scene change?"
"Does your animation match what your storyboard shows?"
If a group is stuck: "Just code one button. Make something happen when Left is pressed. That is enough to start."



10:55–11:30 | Presentations & Peer Applause

Each group gets 3–4 minutes. With 10 groups that is 30–40 minutes — stay firm on timing.

Presentation format — the mini theatre:


The Narrator introduces the story: "Our story is about…"
The Props Manager holds up Panel 1 (Beginning) and moves the character cutout while the Narrator describes it
The Narrator moves through Panel 2 (Middle) and Panel 3 (End) the same way
The Coder runs the Blockly animation live — narrating as they press buttons: "When I press Left, the character moves toward the danger…"
The group explains one concept they used: "We used a loop here… our IF rule says…"


Presentation prompts written on the board:


"Our story is about…"
"In the beginning…  In the middle… At the end…"
"We used a loop to…"
"Our IF rule says: IF ___, THEN ___"
"When the [key] is pressed, our character…"


Peer feedback — 2 Stars and a Wish:
After each group, the class gives:


⭐ "I loved the way you…"
⭐ "Your story was creative because…"
🌠 "I wish you had added…"


Facilitators model this warmly for the very first group. Remind kids: specific is better than general. "I loved your lion cutout" is better than "it was good."


11:30–11:45 | Journal & Reflection

Prompts:


My favourite thing I built this week was ___
One thing I learned that surprised me was ___
I feel proud because ___
The hardest moment was ___, and I got through it by ___
If I could add one more thing to my project, it would be ___
✏️ Draw your favourite moment or character from the whole week



11:45–12:00 | Final Badges & Certificate Moment

🏅 Badge of the Day: "Code Champion"

Earned by:


✅ Completing a cardboard storyboard and character cutout
✅ Building a LEGO scene and a Blockly animation
✅ Presenting to the group
✅ Giving peer feedback to another group


🎓 Certificate:
Print and sign before the day. Call each child by name to receive theirs individually. Write the line on every certificate:
"For coding with courage and creativity — Dream Beyond Limits 2026."

Closing message:


"Five days ago, you didn't know what a loop was. Now you can build one, explain one, and use one to tell a story. You made characters out of cardboard and brought them to life with code. You are Instruction Explorers. Loop Masters. Decision Makers. Creative Coders. Code Champions. That is who you are now — and nobody can take that away from you. We cannot wait to see what you build next."




Volunteer Notes — Day 5


Timing is the main risk. 10 groups × 4 minutes = 40 minutes of presentations. Start on time and keep a visible countdown for each group. A volunteer should be the designated timekeeper — not the facilitator who is watching and encouraging.
The cardboard is the safety net. If a device fails or the Blockly animation isn't working, a group can present entirely through their cardboard mini theatre and describe what their code would do. The thinking is the project. The screen is just one way to show it.
Certificates: Print the night before. Sign them. This matters enormously to children who have never received a formal certificate for anything academic. Take it seriously.
Photographs: If families or community members attend the showcase, agree on a photo policy before the day. Group photos of the work are always safe. Individual child photos need care and, ideally, prior consent from parents.
Cardboard to take home: Every child should leave with their group's character cutout if nothing else. It is a tangible thing they made with their hands. Do not let these get thrown away.
