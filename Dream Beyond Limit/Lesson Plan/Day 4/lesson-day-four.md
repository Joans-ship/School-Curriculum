Day 4 — Animation & Collaboration: "Plan It. Build It. Animate It."

Theme: Together, we bring characters to life — and plan how to tell their story.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of Day 4, kids should be able to:


Understand that animation is a sequence of changes triggered by events
Use Blockly events, movement, costumes, and sounds to animate a character
Work as a team to agree on a story with a clear beginning, middle, and end
Create a cardboard storyboard and physical character cutouts as their final project plan
Carry their plan into Day 5 ready to build and present



10:00–10:15 | Day 3 Recap + Energiser — "If I'm a…"

Kids stand in a circle. Pass an object. Whoever holds it gives a character + conditional combo:


"If I'm a cat → I pounce."
"If I'm a robot → I spin."
"If I'm a lion → I roar."


This warms up creative thinking and reinforces if-then logic as a bridge into storytelling.


10:15–10:35 | Mini Lesson — What Is Animation?

Facilitator talk points:


Animation = making something change over time. A character moves. A scene shifts. A face changes expression.
In code, animation is a sequence of actions triggered by events. Nothing moves until something happens — a key press, a click, a timer.
"What's your favourite animated show or game? What makes it feel alive?" (movement, reaction, sound, colour change)

🚀 How Games Think – Understanding Game Logic! 🎮

Post-watch: "What events did you see? What happened when the player pressed a button?"


10:35–11:05 | Blockly Activity — Story Animator

Kids learn the animation tool they will use for their final project on Day 5. This is the teaching slot — structured levels, no pressure to finish a full story yet.

What the activity should look like:

Kids open the Story Animator. The right side of the screen has a stage — a simple scene with an emoji character. Below the stage are 5 buttons: Left, Right, Up, Down, Space. The workspace has blocks in 5 categories:


Events: "When LEFT pressed," "When RIGHT pressed," "When UP pressed," "When DOWN pressed," "When SPACE pressed"
Move: move left, move right, jump, spin
Speak: say [message] — chosen from a dropdown
Look: change costume to [emoji], change background to [scene name]
Sound: play sound [clap / beep / cheer / woosh]


Kids drag an event block into the workspace, then stack action blocks inside it. Clicking Run loads the code. The character sits idle until a button is pressed — then the coded actions fire.

There are 4 levels:


Level 1: Code Left and Right buttons to move the character across the stage.
Level 2: Add Up (say something) and Down (change costume). Four buttons, four different actions.
Level 3: Add Space to change the background. Character movement + scene change together start to feel like a story.
Level 4: Free mode — all 5 buttons unlocked. Kids experiment freely. This is a preview of the Day 5 sandbox.


Facilitator prompt for every group:


"Press the buttons in order and narrate out loud as it plays. That is exactly what you'll do during your Day 5 presentation."



Facilitator tip: Don't push groups to finish all 4 levels. Levels 1–2 are enough for kids to feel confident going into Day 5. Level 4 is for groups who move fast.


11:05–11:20 | LEGO — "Stack Your Character"

A short, focused LEGO activity that links physical building to the animator concept before the cardboard work begins.

Instructions:
Each group decides on their main character for their final project story. They represent that character as a brick stack — specific colours in a specific order. For example:


Yellow-red-blue (bottom to top) = the robot
Green-green-yellow = the lion


Then they answer three questions by building:


What does the character look like at the start of the story? (one stack)
What does the character look like at the end? (a different or changed stack)
What changed between the two stacks, and why?


This makes the concept of character change — which is the heart of every story — physical and visible before they draw it.

Facilitator question:


"If your character changes from the beginning to the end, what event caused that change? What button would trigger it in your animation?"




11:20–11:55 | Cardboard Activity — Storyboard + Character Cutouts

This is where the final project planning happens. Groups use cardboard, markers, coloured paper, scissors, and stickers to build the physical plan they will present and animate on Day 5.

Volunteer prep: Pre-cut cardboard into panels before class if possible. Each group needs:


3 cardboard panels roughly A4 size (one per scene)
1 smaller piece per character for cutouts
Access to markers, coloured paper, and stickers


Part 1 — 3-Panel Storyboard (approx. 20 minutes)

Each panel represents one scene: Beginning / Middle / End.

On each panel, groups draw and decorate:


The setting — what does this scene look like? Sky, road, forest, space, classroom?
The character(s) — where are they? What are they doing?
A caption — one or two sentences describing what is happening
The event label — written at the bottom: "Event that moves to next scene: [key press / action]"


The panels should be colourful, clear, and large enough to hold up during a presentation. This is their presentation visual — it does not need to be perfect art. It needs to communicate the story.

Part 2 — Character Cutouts (approx. 15 minutes)

Each group cuts out at least one main character from cardboard and decorates it with markers, coloured paper, and stickers. The cutout should:


Be recognisable as the character (a lion, a robot, a child, a spaceship)
Be large enough to hold up and move across the storyboard panels
Have the character's name written on the back


Groups with time can make 2–3 cutouts — a main character and a supporting character or villain.

How the cutouts are used on Day 5: During the presentation, one child holds up the storyboard panel while another physically moves the character cutout across it as the narrator tells the story. It becomes a live mini theatre performance alongside the Blockly animation.

Facilitator tip: Circulate and ask each group: "Where does your character start? Where do they end up? What happens in the middle that changes everything?" If a group can answer those three questions clearly, their story is strong enough to build from.

If groups do not finish: That is completely fine. The cardboard activity continues at the start of Day 5. Groups should aim to finish at least Panel 1 and their main character cutout before the end of Day 4.

Keep this brief — groups need to carry their cardboard home or store it safely overnight.
